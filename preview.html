  Let Each Sphere Come First  
credit: sailko / CC BY 2.5 / Wikimedia Commons  
  Think about how rarely we give ourselves fully to whatever we're doing. Working, some part of us is thinking about the errands we need to run later or the conversation we had the other day. Eating dinner with our families, some part of us is thinking about that email from earlier or that meeting tomorrow. We're in a conversation, we're reading, we're watching a movie, as our minds are off something else.   Marveling at the way Charles de Gaulle, despite the demands of running a country, managed to give himself just as fully to his family, Richard Nixon once wrote, In the Elysee his office was only a few steps across a hall from his residence, but it might as well have been across the continent, because the two were entirely different worlds. His aides knew that de Gaulle was almost completely inaccessible after he left his office at the end of the day. No one was to call him unless there was an emergency. His time with his family was his own and their own...With this separation he struck a balance that few are able to--neither job nor family came second. Each was first in its own sphere.   Marcus Aurelius, who had an empire demanding his attention, similarly reminded himself to concentrate on "doing what's in front of you" and on "freeing yourself from all other distractions." This applies to meetings and meals. To being at work or on a walk. To the person you're talking to and the book you finally have an hour to read.   When it's time to work, settle into the work. When someone is talking, listen. When you've set aside time to rest, allow yourself to enjoy it. Whatever comes next will have its turn. Let what's in front of you, whichever sphere you're in, come first.
